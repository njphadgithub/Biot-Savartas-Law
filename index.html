1. The Right-Hand Grip Rule (Straight Conductors)

Grasp the straight conductor with your right hand such that your outstretched thumb points in the direction of conventional electric current (I). Your four fingers automatically curl around the wire in the exact direction of the concentric magnetic field lines (B).

B = (μ₀ I) / (2π ρ) â_φ

2. The Right-Hand Curl Rule (Circular Loops & Solenoids)

Curl your four right-hand fingers along the circular conductor in the direction of current flow. Your outstretched thumb will point along the axis in the direction of the emerging magnetic flux — identifying the North (N) magnetic pole!

B_axial = (μ₀ I R²) / [2(R² + z²)^(3/2)] â_z

3. Biot-Savart Differential Cross-Product Rule

The magnetic field contribution from a differential current element dL to a field observation point r is strictly governed by the vector cross product: dL × â_r. The resulting dB vector is always mutually perpendicular to both the conductor element and the line of sight.

dB = (μ₀ I / 4π) · (dL × â_r) / r²

Case A: Magnetic Field on the Axis of a Circular Current Loop

Consider a loop of radius R in the xy-plane carrying current I. The position of an element dL is (R cos φ, R sin φ, 0) and the observation point on the z-axis is (0, 0, z). The distance vector r is (-R cos φ â_x - R sin φ â_y + z â_z) with magnitude r = √(R² + z²).

By symmetry, transverse components Bx and By integrate to zero over 0 to 2π. The axial component Bz adds constructively:

B_z = ∫ (μ₀ I dL / 4π r²) · sin θ = (μ₀ I R² / 2) · 1 / (R² + z²)^(3/2)

Case B: Ideal Solenoid Uniform Core Field via Ampere's Law

Applying Ampere's circuital law ∮ B · dL = μ₀ I_enc along a rectangular Amperian loop of length L with one side inside the core and the other outside where B ≈ 0:

B · L = μ₀ (N I) ➔ B = μ₀ n I (where n = N/L)

Case C: Helmholtz Coil Uniform Field Condition

Separating two coaxial loops of radius R by distance d = R causes both the first and second axial derivatives of B to vanish at the midpoint (dB/dz = 0, d²B/dz² = 0), yielding an extraordinarily flat, uniform test zone.

B_center = (4/5)^(3/2) · (μ₀ I / R) ≈ 0.7155 · (μ₀ I / R)

Question 1: Current Reversal & Magnetic Polarity

A circular copper loop in the horizontal plane carries conventional current in the counter-clockwise direction when viewed from above. If the current direction is reversed to clockwise, what happens to the central magnetic field?

Correct! By the Right-Hand Rule, curling fingers clockwise points the thumb downwards (-Z). The North magnetic pole flips to the bottom of the loop.

Question 2: Field Inside a Toroidal Inductor

Why do toroidal coils exhibit virtually zero external electromagnetic leakage compared to straight solenoids?

Correct! The magnetic field lines inside a toroid are closed circles centered on the symmetry axis, giving zero exterior magnetic dipole moment.
